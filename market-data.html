Interconnection queues, capacity additions, and siting rules, by the numbers

Interconnection queue capacity and wait times, solar, wind, and battery capacity additions, and local siting ordinance counts, from published public sources.

Three different kinds of evidence sit behind this name, and they must not be mixed. The interconnection queue counts projects seeking permission to connect; developer plans count projects developers intend to build; and completed additions count projects actually built. The queue is by far the largest of the three — 2,061 GW waiting against roughly 64 GW planned for a single year — and that gap is the point: permission, not ambition, is the scarce resource. A second distinction matters throughout: solar, wind, and storage figures come from different surveys with different definitions, so each row below names its own source and its own basis.

What the latest numbers show is a queue that is shrinking through withdrawals rather than completions, a buildout led overwhelmingly by solar and storage, and a local rulebook that has grown sixfold for wind in four years. The developer who reads all three together — queue, buildout, and ordinance count — is reading the siting market as it actually works.

How to read these numbers

Included: Berkeley Lab Queued Up 2026 queue capacity, composition, movement, agreement status, completion rates, timelines, and regional figures (data through end of 2025); EIA developer plans for 2025 and completed battery additions for 2024, kept in separate labeled rows; SEIA / Wood Mackenzie completed solar installations for Q3 2025; IEA Wind installed wind capacity for end of 2024; NREL local ordinance counts and modeled setback effects; and the FERC Order No. 2023 study timeline as a rule, not an outcome. Excluded: undated homepage claims presented as dated figures, market totals that blend planned and completed capacity, and any queue figure that mixes generation with load interconnection requests.

Forecasts and plans are labeled as forecasts and plans wherever they appear. Queue figures describe projects seeking connection, most of which will never be built, and are shown with their reporting dates so a reader can judge their age.
