The thesis

A name that states the discipline.

Most energy brands sound like generators, installers, or funds. EnergyZoning.com sounds like the work that comes before any of them: finding land, clearing zoning, and getting a project connected.

That bluntness is the point. A developer deciding where to site a 200-megawatt project does not want poetry. The developer wants to know, before anything else, that the platform on the other side of the screen understands setbacks, interconnection studies, and county hearings — and that it covers all three, not just one.

The name is short, pronounceable, and exact. It works on a data dashboard, on a permitting tracker, on a proposal to a county board, and in front of a landowner who needs to know, in one glance, that this is not a generic clean-energy blog. It is a siting brand first.

Specific, and broad enough to travel. A strong energy-siting brand must do three things at once: serve developers, serve the landowners and counties they negotiate with, and travel across states whose rules differ county by county. This name is specific enough to rank in the mind of a developer searching for zoning answers, and broad enough to cover solar, wind, and storage siting, queue tracking, ordinance databases, and permitting workflow from Texas to the Midwest. It avoids jargon that only one market understands, and it avoids vagueness that says nothing at all.
